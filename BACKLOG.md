## Phases, and what each one may say

DECISIONS §130. Two products ship on different tiers, so they gate differently.
The mistake to avoid is treating the **audit** (P0–P4) and the **tint
correction** (A109) as one thing: the first is systematic work that gates phase
2, the second is a day's correction that gates a public claim.

| | Phase 1 — Cave and the wallet | Phase 2 — Zyn is tradable |
|---|---|---|
| What opens | the NFT sale; Nap in the stores | a spendable balance held here |
| Privacy tier | 1 | 4 |
| The sentence | "your items are private from the public; we can see them" | "we cannot see what you hold" |
| Gates | **A90** (sealing — without it "operator-trusted" is untrue, it is public), **A109** (Nap's third visual category), Gate 2 security items | tiers 2–4 (A106–A108), then **P0–P4 as the last gate** |

**Ordering inside phase 1.** A109 comes before the store submission, not after:
a listing is a durable public claim, review takes weeks of calendar rather than
work, and the fix is a day. A90 comes before Cave opens and before any further
epoch is published — it cannot be applied backwards. Nap may reach the stores
while Cave is still closed; the two only share A109.

**P0–P4 is deliberately not a phase-1 gate.** It is the audit that makes a
privacy claim defensible, and phase 1 makes the modest claim §70.1 always
made. Phase 2 makes the strong one and does not open until the audit passes.

## Milestones: the privacy ladder

DECISIONS §130. Zyn's framing for closing the gap between "shielded money" and
"programmable private state". The rung ladder above decentralises the
sequencer; this one blinds it. **Tier 4 gates Zyn as private money** — nobody
holds a spendable balance here under a privacy claim until their amounts and
ownership are hidden from us too. It does **not** gate Cave, which ships at tier
1 as §70.1 always intended (§130.8a), nor ZynZap — though ZynZap's copy must
state the tier it is actually on (§130.8b).

### O1 evidence — the forced-inclusion experiment, 10 Sep 2026

A real forced intent was sent end to end and **was never applied**. What is
established, so the next person does not repeat it:

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Intent | `Transfer` 0.0001 xZEC to account `0202…02`, signed by `ea329c6c…` |
| Sent via | `POST /api/force` on `nap-wallet` — builds the frame, wraps it in a `ZYF` memo, pays 10,000 zat to the vault |
| Zcash txid | `1c7ad023a9be14e5ee5fb06b00b2a8904cc7a205be7a15b127447a0f646be0d7` |
| Confirmed | height **4,337,728**, **12 confirmations** (6 required) |
| Sequencer scanned to | **4,337,734** — past it, reporting `ok` |
| Result | **nothing.** No deposit, no forced sighting, no `Unattributable` error, `seq` unchanged at 11548, `accounts` unchanged at 10 |

**Ruled out**

- The memo layer: `cargo test -p zyn-custody memo` passes, including
  `a_forced_memo_is_neither_a_deposit_nor_an_anchor_and_round_trips_its_frame`
- `ZYN_IGNORE_FORCED`: not set on the sequencer
- The call path: `bridge.rs:456` does call `apply_forced` from the observe loop
- Depth: 12 confirmations against a requirement of 6
- Signers: unrelated — both endorse normally and certificates clear

**Where it must be.** The scanner produced no `ForcedSighting`, so execution never
reached `apply_forced`. In `shielded.rs:250` the sighting requires *both*
`memo::forced_frame(&memo_bytes)` **and** `memo::forced_account(frame)` to return
`Some`, on a note that decrypted as ours. One of those three is failing silently —
and silently is the problem: every other branch there logs or raises.

**Narrowed further, 10 Sep.** A test now drives a *real* submission frame through
the memo round trip — `client.rs::forced_frame_tests` — and it **passes**: the frame
survives `encode_forced` → `forced_frame` unchanged, and `forced_account` recovers
the signer. So the frame layout, the memo wrapper and the signer recovery are all
sound. That leaves exactly two candidates:

1. the note did not decrypt as the vault's (wrong receiver, or wrong pool — the
   sending wallet held **Ironwood** only: `ironwood: 90000, orchard: 0`), or
2. the scanner's range never covered that block in a pass where a sighting could be
   made.

**In flight.** `ZYN_FORCED_RESCAN_FROM=4337720` is set on the sequencer, which makes
`rescan_forced` re-read the range on boot and log how many it queued. Its answer
decides between the two: a non-zero count means the scanner *can* see the memo and
the live pass missed it; zero means the note is not being recognised as the vault's
at all. **Remove that variable once the answer is in** — it re-reads on every boot.

**Why it matters twice.** Rung 1 has only ever been demonstrated *backwards* — the
7 Sep run proved a replica notices censorship. Nothing has ever proved the happy
path. A censorship-resistance mechanism that detects censorship but cannot deliver
an intent is half a mechanism, and it is beat 5 of the demo.

## Known operating characteristics

**Restarting `zynzapd` costs a multi-hour note-tree rebuild.** Observed 21 Sep
on testnet: after a restart the node logs `note trees synced to N of M` and
grinds from its saved position to the chain tip at ~5 blocks/s with one core
pinned. While it runs it serves no status and produces no epochs, so the RPC
goes quiet and the DA directory stops growing — which looks exactly like a
hang. It is not. Check the `synced to` number is advancing over a **two-minute**
window, not thirty seconds; a shorter sample straddles one log line and reads
as stalled.

**It is superlinear, so there is no honest estimate at all.** Measured across
21 Sep: 1.2 blocks/s over the first 2.5 hours, then 0.39, then 0.28, then
twenty-five minutes with no log line — at a constant 99.4% CPU, no iowait, on
a box with 3.8 GB of RAM and ~1 GB of swap in use. Constant CPU with a falling
block rate means the work *per block* is growing. It does not converge on a
deadline; it gets slower the further it goes.

Three estimates were given before this was understood — 47 minutes, 8 minutes,
then 4.6 hours — each extrapolated from a short sample of a decelerating
process. **Do not quote a completion time for this operation.** Report the
position and whether it is still moving.

This is the real cost of any change requiring a sequencer restart, and it is
why an env-only change must be batched with a binary deploy rather than done
separately. **On mainnet the same restart is a multi-hour window with no
anchors** — which, once deposits are open, is a multi-hour window in which no
state root reaches Zcash. Budget it as half a day, not as a coffee break.
